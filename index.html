Questions

Does it rename desktops inside Mission Control?

No. macOS doesn’t let any app change those labels without disabling system protections, and we won’t ask you to do that. Your names appear in the menu bar and in SpaceNamer’s menu, which is where you look anyway.

What permissions does it need?

None. It never asks for Accessibility or Screen Recording, and it doesn’t touch System Integrity Protection.

Will it survive the next macOS update?

It reads desktop information the same way macOS itself does and switches desktops through public window focus, so there’s far less to break than in tools that fake keystrokes. We test each new macOS release the week it ships and publish the result on the compatibility page.

Spaceman and WhichSpace are free. Why pay?

Spaceman caps names at three characters and can’t switch desktops. WhichSpace shows numbers and mirrors one item across every display. If those fit you, use them. SpaceNamer gives you full names, one-click switching, per-display lists, a signed and notarized build, and updates for each macOS release.

How many Macs can I use it on?

Three. Moving to a new Mac? Remove the license on the old one from the License window, or free a seat from the customer portal.

Do my names survive a restart, or reordering desktops?

Yes. Names are tied to each desktop itself, not to its position, so they follow the desktop when Mission Control reorders it or after a restart.

Does it work with multiple monitors?

Yes. The menu shows one list per display with a checkmark on each display’s active desktop.

Will it clutter my menu bar?

It’s one item showing the current desktop’s name, and it replaces a trip to Mission Control. Long names are shortened, and it works alongside menu bar managers like Bartender.

Intel Macs?

No. SpaceNamer runs on Apple silicon Macs with macOS 13 or later.

Can I turn off the switching animation?

Not today. SpaceNamer uses macOS’s own switch, animation included.

Is it notarized? What happens when I download it?

Yes, it’s signed and notarized by Apple. Unzip, move it to Applications, open it. No warnings, no right-click tricks. It updates itself from then on.
